<h2>Introduction: COVID-19 in the context of Socialism</h2>
<p>Capitalism puts profit above all else, even our very lives. In a recent interview, the Mayor of Las Vegas suggested that Las Vegas businesses be reopened and the city of Las Vegas be used as a “control group” for social distancing. With the ongoing threat of the virus, it has become clear that reopening businesses and easing social distancing restrictions in this manner will directly lead to an increased number of cases of COVID-19, hospitalizations due to the virus, and ultimately an increase in the number of deaths. So why even entertain the thought of easing restrictions that are literally saving lives?</p>
<p>The ruling class has made it clear that it doesn&#8217;t care about anyone else’s safety, wellbeing, or life as long as it can continue to profit from our labor. However, capitalism is <i>not</i> inevitable. In SVDSA, we are working towards a world where everyone’s basic shelter, food, and healthcare needs are met. We are working towards a world where workers have democratic control of their own workplaces, and where regular people can live in and democratically participate in society, and where our lives and the well-being of the planet are valued more than “the economy.” We care a lot about our community, and invite you to join us in supporting people in the midst of this crisis.</p>

<h2>FAQ</h2>
<p><em>How to know if you or a loved one has COVID-19:</em></p>
<ul>
<li>According to WHO, the most common symptoms are fever, dry cough, and tiredness, but some also experience aches and pains, nasal congestion, sore throat, or diarrhea.</li>
<li>However, some experience mild or almost no symptoms and can still transmit the virus, so it’s still important to be safe and social distance.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>How does COVID-19 spread?</em></p>
<ul>
<li>The virus spreads through airborne droplets exhaled by an infected person, which can land on objects or other people.</li>
<li>You may contract COVID-19 if you touch one of these objects and then touch your face, or if you breathe in droplets in the air.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>To prevent the spread of COVID-19:</em></p>
<ul>
<li>Wear a face mask when you leave your home</li>
<li>Refrain from touching your face as much as possible and keep at least three feet away from anyone infected.</li>
<li>Wash your hands frequently with soap or clean them with an alcohol-based hand rub.</li>
<li>Stay inside! If you’re feeling unwell, you don’t want to spread COVID-19. If you aren’t, some can be asymptomatic carriers and you don’t want to catch COVID-19, so still stay home.</li>
<li>Stay informed with <a href="https://www.sccgov.org/sites/covid19/Pages/home.aspx">local</a> updates regarding COVID-19. Local authorities have the most information about what’s going on in your area.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>What to do if you experience symptoms:</em></p>
<ul>
<li>If you experience mild symptoms, you probably don’t need to visit a hospital and will recover if you self-isolate and follow safety guidelines like wearing a mask.</li>
<li>If you experience severe symptoms, especially chest pain, fever, or difficulty breathing, you should seek medical attention.</li>
<li>If you visit a hospital, call ahead so that you can be directed to the correct facility. Remember to wear a mask and avoid touching surfaces as much as possible, since the virus can survive for up to 72 hours on stainless steel, 4 hours on copper, and 24 hours on cardboard.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>Who should wear a mask:</em></p>
<ul>
<li>Healthcare workers</li>
<li>Anyone who exhibits symptoms of COVID-19</li>
<li>Anyone who is a caretaker of someone exhibiting symptoms of COVID-19</li>
<li>Anyone out in a public space</li>
</ul>
<p><em>How to wear a mask:</em></p>
<ul>
<li>Before putting a mask on:
<ul>
<li>Wash your hands.</li>
<li>Inspect it for any holes or tears.</li>
<li>Make sure that it’s oriented properly (if there is a metal strip, it should be on top, and the colored side should face outwards).</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>When applying the mask:
<ul>
<li>Make sure the stiff top edge covers your nose and molds to its shape.</li>
<li>Make sure the bottom edge covers your mouth.</li>
<li>Test the mask seal by covering it lightly with both hands and breathing out. No air should escape the mask.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>When wearing the mask:
<ul>
<li>Don’t touch it! This can transmit germs, viruses, etc straight to your nose and mouth.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>When removing the mask:
<ul>
<li>Wash your hands.</li>
<li>Avoid touching the mask itself, and hold onto the elastics on your ears instead. Keep the mask away from your face and clothes.</li>
<li>Throw it away, don’t reuse it if you can (if you are going to reuse a cloth mask, make sure to wash it thoroughly between uses).</li>
<li>After touching a mask, always wash your hands.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><em>If you go out:</em></p>
<ul>
<li>The WHO recommends keeping at least 1 meter (3 feet) away from others in order to prevent virus transmission, and the CDC recommends keeping at least 6 feet apart, even if you’re wearing a mask.</li>
<li>Avoid going out at peak times for traffic. If you have to get groceries, consider going in the early morning or later at night.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>If you have pets:</em></p>
<ul>
<li>Though the virus may have originated in an animal, there still is no evidence that animals who are infected with COVID-19 are capable of transmitting it to humans.</li>
<li>Still, avoid contact with pets as much as possible, and wash your hands after you handle them.</li>
</ul>
